# Writing — emails, messages, documents, reports

Read when drafting or editing anything a person will read: email, chat message, post, doc, report, proposal, README, summary.

## Before writing — settle silently

```
Reader     who reads it, what they already know, how much time they'll give it
Purpose    the one thing the reader should know, decide, or do after reading
Register   the user's relationship to the reader — boss, client, team, public
Language   the language the user will send it in (the request's language unless told otherwise)
```

Missing a fact the draft can't work without (a date, a name, an amount) → leave a clear placeholder `[new due date]` and say so in one line. Never invent facts to fill a gap.

## The draft

- **Deliver the finished text**, ready to send — not an outline, not tips on how to write it.
- **Lead with the point.** First sentence = the ask or the news. Background after, only as much as the reader needs.
- **One ask per message** where possible; make it concrete (what, by when).
- **Requests and bad news** carry: what, why (one line), the new date or option, and what is being done meanwhile.
- **Email**: subject line that states the point ("Request to move Project X delivery to Oct 24"), greeting and sign-off in the user's usual register.
- **Documents and reports**: headings that say something, summary first, numbers with units and dates, decisions and owners explicit.
- **Register and politeness**: match the user's own voice — polite particles, honorifics and formality as they would write them; no machine-translated phrasing; technical terms stay in English when that's how the team speaks.
- **Long or high-stakes pieces** may be split across helper models (drafts on a balanced model, critique on a different one, the last pass mine): `references/delegation.md` *Choosing the model and effort* §3. A short message stays mine alone.

## Editing someone's text

Keep their voice and structure; change only what improves clarity, accuracy, or tone. Report what changed in one line per category, not a line-by-line diff, unless asked.

## After the draft

At most one line: a placeholder to fill or one choice for the user (e.g. formal vs. friendly). No "let me know if you want changes".
