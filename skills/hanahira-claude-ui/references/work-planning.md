# Work planning — projects, priorities, schedules, meetings, status

Read when the user wants to plan work, prioritize, schedule, run or summarize a meeting, or report status. (Creating something whose shape isn't settled yet — a new product, talk, feature: `planning.md` first.)

## A plan

1. **Restate the goal and the hard limits** in one or two lines — deadline, people or hours available, budget, what can't break.
2. **Tasks**, each with: what "done" looks like (a check, not a feeling), owner, and an order or date. Tasks sized so each fits in a day or less; bigger → split.
3. **Order by dependency, then by risk**: do the risky or unknown parts early, when there's still time to react.
4. **Critical path**: the chain that sets the end date — name it.
5. **Top 2 risks** with the early warning sign and the fallback.
6. **Next action on top**: the first concrete step the user can do today.

Fit the plan to the capacity stated. Working alone 2 hours a day for 2 weeks ≈ 20 hours — if the tasks don't fit, say what to cut or move, don't pretend.

## Prioritizing

Impact × urgency, then effort. Say what's **not** being done and why — an unranked list of "important" items isn't a priority.

## Meetings

- **Before**: purpose in one line, decisions needed, attendees who own them, time-boxed agenda.
- **After / summarizing notes**: `Decisions` · `Action items — owner, due date` · `Open questions`. Only what the notes support; mark unclear owners or dates `[not stated]` (in the user's language) rather than guessing.

## Status updates

```
Done      what finished since last update (with the evidence)
Next      what happens next, by when
Blocked   what's stuck and what or who unblocks it
Risk      anything that may move the date
```

Lead with whether it's on track. Bad news early, with the new date and the fix.

## Output format

Plain lists or a table in chat. If the user said they'll keep, share, or track it, make it as the right artifact (task list, doc, or sheet).
